**Religion & Deities** examines the myriad supernatural and extraplanar beings recognized throughout [[The Mundane World of Eruven|Eruven]], including deities, celestials, fey entities, demons, devils, and other powers whose true existence lies beyond the mortal planes. This chapter documents their origins, attributes, spheres of influence, and the varied ways in which mortal peoples venerate, bargain with, or fear them.
## Religion
*For specific information on this topic, see [[Religions and Pantheons]]*

Despite the fact that the gods are objectively real, most people of the people of [[The Mundane World of Eruven|Eruven]], regardless of ancestry, do not believe the gods control everything that happens in the world. Rather, they believe the gods created a natural world with its own cycles that are sometimes predictable, but which cannot be understood. 

When a river floods or crops fail, some people may curse the gods, but those people do not all believe a god caused the events. They may curse simply because the gods did not prevent catastrophe. They do not think, "Why did the gods do that?" They think, "Why did the gods make a world that behaves like this?"

The gods are powerful, but forbidden from acting directly upon or within the Mundane World by [[The Law of Nature|the Law of Nature]]. To enact their will, they rely on intermediaries, beings of demigod-like status, once mortals, who now serve their patron god in eternity. Many [[Humans|humans]] call these beings [[Saints & Legendary Heroes|saints]], while other humanoid peoples refer to them as heroes, or legendary heroes.

Each god has many saints, some obscure. They occasionally manifest in the world, but these encounters are always brief, leading sages and theologians to conclude there must be some limit to how much time or energy these saints and legendary heroes can spend on the Mundane Plane. 

As a result of this complex tapestry of belief and personalities, a farmer in [[Edrathen]] might call upon [[Adûn]] for strength while tilling the earth. But if a cool breeze suddenly arose, easing the sun's heat upon their back, they would almost certainly not thank Adûn or any of his saints. Instead, they might thank [[Saint Elspeth the Blithe]], sometimes called the Summer Breeze. If they had a neighbor or relative with a shrine to Saint Elspeth in their home, they might give that person a small gift or offering and ask them to place it on their shrine. More likely, they would silently promise to do this, and then forget or get distracted.

If questioned about this, "Why did you call upon one god but thank the saint of a completely different god?", they would first be surprised by the question. It would not have occurred to them to examine this behavior. If pressed they would say, "Oh, I'm sure they worked it out between them." They would not imagine, "Adûn told [[Viras]], who told her saint, Elspeth, to send a cooling breeze," but they would assume something like that happened and not worry overmuch about the details. They called out and they received aid, that is what's important.
### Churches and Temples
Most religions in Eruven organize themselves into hierarchies and build temples or cathedrals. Others exist as little more than traditions handed down from shaman to shaman, or wise-man to apprentice.

Because the gods are forbidden from acting directly upon the world, churches and temples in Eruven are consecrated to saints, or heroes. There are no temples to [[Eruv]] or churches of [[Cavall]]. Instead, there is the Church of [[Zarok the Law-Giver]] and the Church of [[Saint Llewellyn the Valliant]]. Everyone knows which god a particular saint or hero serves; it is common knowledge. A human in [[Corvallis]] might call out to Cavall for aid in times of need, but they know that, should Cavall chose to help, it will be by sending one of his saints.

The most popular saints and heroes can have several churches, devoted to them, each with their own rituals. In [[Veylan]], there is the Church of Saint Llewellyn the Valliant, but there is also the smaller Church of [[Saint Llewellyn the Charitable]] with only a few temples.

Usually, these churches recognize each other as expressing different, equally valid teaching of the same saint, but occasionally churches compete to see which among them is the "true" church. The saints don't discourage this, and thus must, in some sense, approve.
## Deities
Given the nature of these beings, the accounts presented herein must be regarded as interpretations rather than incontrovertible fact. Creation myths, divine hierarchies, and descriptions of the [[The Planar Expanse|planes]] often differ dramatically between cultures and sources, reflecting the limitations of those who attempt to perceive the ineffable. Particular attention is therefore given to competing traditions, regional variations in worship, and the historical consequences of divine or infernal intervention upon mortal affairs.

Some of these beings exist as singular, transcendent powers. Others command vast courts or hierarchies of lesser spirits and servitors. To begin, we shall examine the highest order of divine powers: [[The Gods|the Gods]]. Among them, there are two categories: the [[The Elder Creator Gods|Elder Creator Gods]] and the innumerable younger [[The Successor Gods|Successor Gods]] worshiped mainly by [[Humans|humans]].

Beyond these principal beings, the many planes of existence are host to numerous extraplanar forces and lesser powers. In order to present them with clarity, the remaining entities have been organized into categories based on their nature, sphere of influence, and relationship to the mortal world.

- [[The Fey Courts]]; ancient, capricious powers connected to the natural areas of Eruven.
- [[The Lords of Hell]]; devils, pact-makers, and the architects of rigid hierarchy in the [[The Seven Cities of Hell|Seven Cities of Hell]].
- [[The Celestials]]; the true elves, denizens of [[Lux the Moon of Dawn]] and [[Nox the Moon of Dusk]], children of [[Tel]], and the creators of the [[Elves|elves]].
- [[The Abyssal Princes]]; demons, creatures of incarnate hate, denizens of the [[Abyssal Wasteland]].
- [[Other Extraplanar Powers]]; entities whose origins or motives defy reliable categorization.
